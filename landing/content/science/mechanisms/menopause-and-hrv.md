## What happens to HRV around menopause?

Menopause is the end of menstrual periods, usually around midlife, after a transition of several years. [Heart rate variability](/glossary/heart-rate-variability) (HRV), the natural variation in time between heartbeats, also changes over these years. In short: HRV falls with age in everyone, differences between women and men fade later in life, and whether menopause itself changes HRV, or whether hot flushes do, is not yet clear [S1, S2].

This page explains what studies have found. It is not medical advice and does not replace care for menopausal symptoms.

## How does it work?

Hot flushes are thought to involve changes in the autonomic nervous system, which also shapes HRV, so researchers have looked for an HRV signature of them. A systematic review found no difference in HRV between women with and without hot flushes [S1]. At the same time, HRV changes with age in nearly every measure, and the differences between women and men disappear in the later decades [S2]. Because menopause and ageing happen together, their effects on HRV are hard to separate; more on age is in [HRV and age](/science/mechanisms/hrv-and-age).

## How is it measured?

Studies record short resting ECGs and compare groups: women with and without hot flushes, women and men of different ages, or women with and without hormone therapy [S1, S2, S3]. Hot flushes are usually self-reported. Recording length, setting, breathing and analysis all affect HRV values, which is one reason the pooled studies disagreed [S4].

## What affects it?

Age is the biggest factor that can be measured [S2]. Sleep is often disrupted around menopause and also moves HRV. Hormone therapy is one question researchers have asked: in a small group of post-menopausal women, it did not change how HRV related to heart rate [S3]. How HRV changes across the menstrual cycle before menopause is covered in [Menstrual cycle and HRV](/science/mechanisms/menstrual-cycle-and-hrv).

## What does the evidence show?

**Age and sex: context-dependent.** In a large population sample, HRV changed with age in nearly all measures, and differences between women and men disappeared in the last two age decades studied [S2].

**Hot flushes: emerging.** A systematic review found no difference in HRV between women with and without hot flushes [S1]. Only three studies could be pooled, they varied widely, and the authors call for larger, standardized studies [S1].

**Hormone therapy: emerging.** In a small exploratory group, hormone therapy did not change the link between HRV and heart rate [S3]. Whether it changes HRV itself has not been shown.

**Unknown.** Whether menopause lowers HRV beyond the effect of age, and whether HRV adds anything to understanding heart risk after menopause.

## What it does not tell you

- **Not a menopause test.** HRV cannot tell whether you are in the menopausal transition.
- **Not a measure of hot flushes.** No HRV difference was found between women with and without them [S1].
- **Not a check of hormone therapy.** HRV has not been shown to reflect whether therapy is working [S3].
- **Not age-free.** A lower HRV in midlife may simply reflect age [S2].
- **When to get help.** Heavy or irregular bleeding, bleeding after menopause, chest pain, palpitations with dizziness, or symptoms that disrupt daily life need a doctor.
